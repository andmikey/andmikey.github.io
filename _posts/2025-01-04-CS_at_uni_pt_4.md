This is the final part of my four-part series about my experience doing an undergraduate degree in Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science at the University of Edinburgh from 2017 to 2022. 

I'll use this post to pull together a few final thoughts about university that I didn't feel were substantial enough to get their own post. 

## Choosing a university

My choice of university came down to two options: I had an offer for the BSc in AI + CS at Edinburgh, or the BA in CS at Cambridge [^1]. I did an analysis table that looked like the below to make my final decision, and went for Edinburgh in the end:

|                        | **Edinburgh**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | **Cambridge**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Academics**          | + Massive amount of course options, particularly in AI/ML/NLP<br>+ Can take courses outside of CS<br>+ Entry conditions (AAA) trivial to meet in year 13<br><br>- Four years for undergrad<br>- Courses likely to be less academically rigorous | + More academically rigorous courses<br>+ Finish undergrad in three years<br><br>- Way fewer course choices available<br>- No opportunity to take non-CS courses<br>- Very intense 8-week terms<br>- Entry conditions tricky to meet (A\*A\*A) |
| **Cost**               | + Tuition fees fully covered by Scottish government (graduate with no student debt)<br>+ Possible to work part-time to help cover living costs during term                                                                                           | - Charged full UK fees (graduate with minimum £40k of debt [^2])<br>- No part-time work during term, breaks between terms likely spent studying                                                                                                   |
| **Living**             | + Accommodation cheap, central, and available on a full-year lease                                                                                                                                                                                           | - College of choice (Girton) very far from city centre<br>- Not possible to stay in accommodation outside of term time<br>- Accommodation much more expensive than Edinburgh                                                                 |
| **Study abroad**       | + Opportunity to study abroad for a full year [^3]                                                                                                                                                                                                        | - No study abroad available                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Career development** | + More time to find and do internships                                                                                                                                                                                                          | - Fewer summers for internships<br>- Unlikely to have much time for internship hunting                                                                                                                                                       |


There were three things which I should have considered in hindsight:
- **Quality of cohort**: I was disappointed by how many students I met at Edinburgh that were just going through the motions of getting the degree rather than fully engaging with the intellectual challenge of it. The handful of students I've met from Cambridge seemed a lot more motivated academically, but it's hard to generalize. 
- **Prestige**: this is fairly nebulous, but I have had a few instances in my career so far where having the Cambridge name would have been useful on my CV. 
- **Contact time with staff**: Cambridge would have had a lot more contact time with staff - from tutorials or otherwise - which I think I would have really enjoyed, because I loved the small-group tutorials I did at Edinburgh. 

I'm still pretty happy with the choice of Edinburgh but I do think sometimes how different things would have been if I'd gone to Cambridge. 


## What I would have done differently

I'm pretty happy with how university went overall. I did well academically, took a lot of interesting courses, graduated with a good CV.

There are a few things I would have changed:

- **More relationships with staff**: I didn't realize how important recommendation letters would be for possible MSc / PhD applications and didn't build any strong relationships with professors who'd be able to write them.
- **Less time studying**: I spent way too much time trying to eke out high grades in courses that didn't interest me and weren't relevant to what I wanted to do. I could have easily halved the amount of time I spent studying and still gotten a first, instead focusing on depth on courses that I found particularly interesting or on more extracurricular activities.
- **Make more risky choices**: I wish I hadn't played it so safe: I could have taken courses I probably wouldn't have gotten good grades in, spent a summer doing a startup or travelling, applied to study abroad somewhere more interesting than the US, that sort of thing. 
- **Make more non-CS friends**: the majority of my friendship group was CS students; I wish I'd put more effort into going outside those social groups and met folks with completely different perspectives. 
- **Explore Scotland more**: Scotland is a beautiful country but I spent so much time studying that I didn't have time to explore it - in the end I only briefly visited Dundee and Glasgow. 

## What next?

I'm going to spend the next few years working my way up in industry, but I expect I'll go back for a Masters at some point. I enjoyed the academic side of uni a bit too much :)
