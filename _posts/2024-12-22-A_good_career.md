I've spent a lot of time in the last few years thinking about what a 'good career' means to me. I've decided it means three things:

1. Working on important problems
2. With large scale impact
3. And having a good time doing it 

I found [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/) very useful reading for developing my thoughts in these areas: I'd recommend reading through their website if you're asking yourself the same questions. 

## Important problems

If I'm spending 40+ hours a week working on something, I want to feel that work has a positive impact on the world.

I found the [80,000 Hours list](https://80000hours.org/career-guide/world-problems/) a helpful starting point, but there are plenty of other things I consider important and impactful enough to motivate me. 

Right now, the sweet-spot of "my interests" and "problems I think are important" is:
- Using AI (or tech more generally) to prevent harms. Fraud prevention falls under this, but I'm interested in finding more examples. 
- Regulating harms caused by commercial use of AI (privacy breaches, political misinformation, encouraging digital addiction)

This broadly seems to come under "responsible and accountable AI" - I need to do more research to see what other opportunities there are in this area. 

I considered maximizing my income and [earning to give](https://80000hours.org/articles/earning-to-give/) as the best path to solving important problems, but I decided it wasn't for me: 
- I felt most high-earning careers that I had a chance in achieving would be [net-harmful](https://80000hours.org/articles/harmful-career/) (or at the very least, net-uncomfortable-to-justify).
- I felt there would be too much disconnect between the day-to-day work and the impact I'm having. I'm most motivated when I can point to something and say "I did that!". 

## Large scale impact

I feel a lot more motivated by my work if I know it's having impact on important problems at a *large scale*.

This is very much "I know it when I see it", so I haven't thought too carefully about what does / doesn't come under this. In my current job I build systems that impact ~millions of people a year, which is a good example of what I'm looking for under this criteria. 

I think one of the reasons I haven't enjoyed working on research in the past is that it comes under "too low impact" - if you're working on a subset of a subset of a problem with a long route to commercialization / productionization, it's hard to be motivated by the possible future impact. 

## Having a good time

None of the above is worth much if I can't stand doing my job. 

Working environment is important to me. That means, I think, the same as it does to most other people:

- Coworkers that I enjoy working with. 
- A feeling of progression and challenge. 
- Being paid enough that I don't need to worry about money. 
- A low-stress environment.  
- Reasonable working hours.
