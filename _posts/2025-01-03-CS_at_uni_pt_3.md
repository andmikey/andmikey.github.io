This is part three of my four-part series about my experience doing an undergraduate degree in Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science at the University of Edinburgh from 2017 to 2022. 

As part of my quest to get very employable at university, I tried to do a lot of extracurricular activities (my mantra was "one CV-worthy activity per semester"). I figured this would also help me figure out what I wanted to do after uni. I've listed out here all the activities I can remember doing. I've definitely missed some out, but those were clearly not important enough to remember! 

## First year
- I went to Glasgow for **JP Morgan's Introduction to Data Science and Machine Learning in Python**, run by Cambridge Spark and attended by plenty of JPMC recruiters. Unfortunately I found out at this event they wouldn't be willing to interview me for a data science internship until I was in penultimate year, so I don't know why they invited me to this in first year. 
- I went to London for the **Facebook Analytics Academy**, a day-long insight into data science and analytics at Facebook. Again, they weren't willing to interview me for internships until penultimate year, but I met some interesting people here.  
- I made an attempt at going to a hackathon, **HackTheBurgh**, but I realized I don't like the vibes of overnight hackathons. 
- **Bloomberg** ran an on-campus **CV review and interview prep** workshop. I got an internship interview out of this, but they expected more knowledge of data structures and algorithms than I had, so I flunked it quite badly. 
- I went to the **Dundee Public Data Hack**, spending two days exploring public datasets using data science tools. I really enjoyed this, and it was nice to explore Dundee (I had very little time to explore Scotland otherwise).  
- In the summer I attended the **IDM Creative Data Academy**, a three-day workshop on the use of data in marketing. This was way less technical than I expected it to be and I didn't feel I got much out of it. 
- I attended a handful of on-campus talks and seminars, mostly run by the Informatics department. 

## Second year
- I went to London for **McKinsey Discover Digital**, a one-day insight into their digital consulting practice, but was completely put off by how heavy the workload expectation was. 
- I also got a taste of consulting by participating in **McKinsey Solve It** (a mock consulting project done over a few weeks in a team of students at my uni), and **FreshSight** (an on-campus society doing pro-bono consulting work for local non-profits), and figured out I didn't like the day-to-day of strategy consulting.
- I attended the **McKinsey QuantumBlack** hackathon and met some very smart folks working there. I did end up interviewing for an internship in the Warsaw office but had to withdraw from the process because of other life stuff that was going on. 
- I went to a **Business Mentors** event, an evening of business skill development (interviewing and giving presentations, with plenty of feedback given) and networking with local businesspeople. I met some folks there who I'm still in touch with today and have been very helpful in my career development.
- I won [**CEOx1Day UK**](https://www.odgersberndtson.com/join-us/ceox1day-uk/) and spent a day shadowing the CEO of DFS (the sofa company) at their head offices in Doncaster. This was a great opportunity to get an insight into what it takes to run a company with £billions in revenue. 
- I attended **3 Day Startup**, a three-day-long workshop where I worked on a team to develop a startup idea. My team didn't get very far with our idea but working through the ideation process was something new to me.  
- I volunteered as a mentor at **Hello World Hack**, a hackathon aimed at beginners. I found the vibe here a lot nicer than HackTheBurgh, and being a mentor was a much better experience than being a participant. The team I mentored won a prize, which was a great feeling.
- Once again I attended a handful of on-campus seminars run by the Informatics department.

## Third year

Covid hit in the middle of my third year, which meant a lot of events got cancelled or didn't run, but I still managed to do a few things:
- I went to Glasgow for **an insight in patent law at Marks and Clerk**. Law is something I've been interested in for a while, and there seems to be a lot of demand for patent lawyers in ML/AI, so I was toying with applying for patent law after graduation. The work itself seemed interesting enough, but I was really put off by having to spend four to six years in training (at a trainee salary!) and sit a bunch of exams before I'd be qualified. 
- I was selected for the **Saltire Scholars** program (which funds internships abroad for students at Scottish universities, and arranges visas), intending to do a tech internship in Silicon Valley. Unfortunately when Covid hit all the international placements were cancelled so this fell through. 
- I became a **student representative**, which meant attending weekly meetings with senior school staff to communicate and resolve issues with the course. I loved being a student rep and I wish I'd started earlier. It's a great way to build relationships with staff and to get an insight into how the department works.  
- I attended **HBS Virtual Peek Weekend**, a short insight into MBA life at HBS (thinking I might apply for the 2+2 program) but it didn't make sense for my career plans and it was _really_ expensive. 
- I attended **ORGCon Scotland**, a day-long conference on digital rights with talks about data privacy and ethics. 

## Gap year

I took a gap year between third and fourth year. I worked full-time for most of this, but I did find time for two activities:
- I **taught myself Swedish to B2** in ~three months while I was working in Sweden.
- I **mentored first-year Informatics students**: this sort of thing would usually happen organically from on-campus interactions, but Covid meant a lot of students missed out on that, so I put a lot of effort into building an online community that would give them a similar experience. For this I received the university-wide **Sense of Belonging Award**. 

## Fourth year

I took it a lot more chill in fourth year, because I was feeling very burnt out:
- I continued being a **student representative** and was the undergraduate student rep on the Board of Studies (policy on delivery of taught courses in Informatics), Internal Periodic Review (review of School of Informatics by an external board), and CSE Curriculum Approval Board (approval of new taught provision across the entire College of Science and Engineering).
- I was a **student ambassador** for open days at the School of Informatics, talking to prospective students about the department. 
- I was **fourth year representative at CompSoc**, the university's computing society. I had been involved in the society since before I even joined the uni, and it was nice to get involved more formally (even if fourth year rep doesn't do much). 
- I picked up some **new hobbies**. I got a bike and cycled around Edinburgh and the surroundings, learned how to cross-stitch, got good at making kimchi, and read a lot of books.
