I read 30 books in 2024. Here are my favourites:

**Non-fiction**

- I read some books about Central Asia. Christopher Robbins' **"In Search of Kazakhstan"** and Erika Fatland's **"Sovietistan"** and **"The Border"** were the highlights. As a result I'm thinking of doing a tour around Central Asia in 2025 or 2026. 
- I followed that up with **"What's Cooking in the Kremlin"** by Witold Szabłowski, which goes through the last 100 years of Russian history from a culinary lens. This led me to **"Nasz mały PRL"** (Polish, Our Little Polish People's Republic; not available in English) by the same author, where he and his family spend 6 months recreating the communist Poland of 1981/1982. 
- The BBC radio shipping forecast hits its 100-year anniversary in 2025, so I read Charlie Connelly's **"Attention All Shipping: A Journey Round the Shipping Forecast"**, a wonderful travelogue where the author travels to every shipping area in the forecast. 
- I'm not usually a fan of Bill Bryson (he comes off as a really mean-spirited person in his travel books), but **"At Home: A Short History of Private Life"** was surprisingly enjoyable; it turns out I quite like his writing when he's not writing about himself.
- Andrew Ross Sorkin's **"Too Big to Fail: The Inside Story of How Wall Street and Washington Fought to Save the Financial System from Crisis — and Themselves"** (what a mouthful!), the insider story of the 2008 financial crisis. A great synthesis of a complex topic (though I did have Wikipedia open as a reference for parts of it). 
- Not quite narrative books, but I spent this year trying to get better at cooking: Samin Nosrat's **"Salt Fat Acid Heat"** led me to Harold McGee's **"On Food and Cooking"**. I also finally bought a copy of Sandor Katz's **"The Art of Fermentation"**.

**Fiction** 

- I finished reading Jonathan Stroud's **"Lockwood and Co"** series: a great young-adult fantasy with a strong ending.
- I happened upon a copy of **"Allt jag inte minns"** (Swedish, Everything I Don't Remember; available in English) by Jonas Hassen Khemiri, a kind of narrative mystery about the unexplained death of a young man. Hit quite hard. 
- Han Kang won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature, so I read **"Greek Lessons"**, which explores language and human nature. I intend to read The Vegetarian this year. 
- I re-read Shion Miura's **"The Easy Life in Kamusari"** and **"Kamusari Tales Told at Night"**, a cozy slice-of-life series about a high schooler training for a career in forestry. The film adaptation (Wood Job!) is good too, though a very different vibe. 
- Jenny Erpenbeck's **"Gehen, ging, gegangen"** (German; Going, Went, Gone), written in response to the refugee crisis in Europe. Unsubtle at times, but I suppose that was the point. I tried reading this in German a few years ago and really struggled with it because it seemed to be loads of jumbled, disconnected thoughts, and I thought I was just misinterpreting the German. This time around I tried it in English. Turns out that's actually how it's written and once I got used to the style it's quite a pleasant read. I'm going to try to read Kairos and Heimsuchung this year.
