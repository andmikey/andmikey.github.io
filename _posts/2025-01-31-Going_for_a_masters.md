I rather enjoyed my time at university, and I'd like to go back for a Masters one day. I've been taking some graduate-level courses for fun recently, and it's got me thinking about what I'd want to get out of a Masters degree and where I'd want to go. 

## The value of a Masters

My main reasons to get a Masters would be, in order:
- Learning: fairly obvious, but I'd like to get a dedicated period of time to study and go deep on topics I didn't cover during undergrad.
- Taking a break: a year or two to study is a nice breather from the 'corporate world'. 
- Signalling: having a masters is a soft requirement for a *lot* of positions of data science. 
- Pivot: studying in another country or another subject gives an opportunity to shift roles/geographies.
- Brand name: going to a 'known' university can help with recruiting for new roles. 

The big drawback of doing one is the cost. I'd be looking at:
1. A year or two of lost earnings and pension contributions
2. A year or two of career progression opportunities lost
3. The additional costs of tuition and subsistence

For those reasons, I'd only be interested in a Masters where:
- The program itself is fully funded (i.e. the additional costs - 3 above - are zero or near-zero).
- I can justify the total cost of the program (1 + 2 + 3 above) versus what I think I'll gain from it in terms of personal, career, and academic development. 

## Where to go

The first question is how to find a funded program. In terms of funding, there are two options:
1. Go to the US or Canada and get a TA/RA role which waives tuition and pays enough to cover my living costs. 
2. Get a scholarship.  

Let's look at each of these in turn.

### TAships / RAships

People on the internet have helpfully put together lists of MSCS courses that have TAship/RAship funding e.g. [here](https://www.fullyfundedweb.com/ms-computer-science.php), [here](https://old.reddit.com/r/MSCS/comments/1ie8dh6/funding_and_scholarships_tuition_waivers_for_rata/), and [here](https://old.reddit.com/r/MSCS/comments/zmohcv/which_uscanadian_universities_offer_good_amount/). 

*However* I'm not very keen on relying on TAship/RAship to fund my studies. Most of them expect 15-20 hours a week of work on top of the degree. I worked that much on top of my undergrad: it's a lot of work and a major distraction from academics. I'd rather avoid this route.  

### Scholarships 

This one is trickier. Masters degrees are money makers for most universities: they're looking to get your money, not give you money. Most scholarships I've seen are *external* to the university. 

I've found the following scholarship / funding programs which I think look feasible:
- The [Thouron Award](https://www.thouronaward.org/), tenable only at the University of Pennsylvania. 
- The [Chinese Government Scholarship](https://yz.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/info/1027/1117.htm), which I'd look to take at Tsinghua. 
- The [Fulbright Scholarship](https://fulbright.org.uk/our-programmes/fulbright-scholarships-to-the-usa/).
    - This covers $66,000 in total, which would suffice for tuition fees for a one-year Masters, but not living costs - and certainly not a two-year Masters. 
    - I would have to return to the UK after completing the degree - I wouldn't be able to use OPT to work in the US afterwards. 
- The [DAAD Master's Scholarship](https://www2.daad.de/deutschland/stipendium/datenbank/en/21148-scholarship-database/?detail=50026200), which pays a stipend of ~1000 EUR per month for Masters degree studies.
    - This must be taken within 6 years of graduation from undergrad, so I would have to start by 2027 (unlikely). 
    - I would look to take this to study the Elite Masters in Software Engineering at the University of Augsburg / Technical University of Munich
- [EIT Digital Masters scholarships](https://masterschool.eitdigital.eu/admissions/fees) - the EU "Scholarships of Excellence" waive tuition and provide a monthly living allowance. 

## Aside: online masters

All of the above assumes studying on-campus. The alternative would be doing an online masters. This could be a good option - I could live somewhere cheap and take courses at my own pace.

The options I'd consider are:
- Georgia Tech's [OMSCS](https://omscs.gatech.edu/) - $195 per credit hour, 30 total hours = $6000 plus fees
- UT Austin's [MSCS](https://cdso.utexas.edu/mscs) - $10,000 


## So... what next?

I'm not doing anything, for the time being. I want to spend some more time in industry first and build up the savings I burned through attending my undergrad. I also want to wait for the political situation in the US to settle down, since most of my best options are in the US and now is not a good time to move over there.
